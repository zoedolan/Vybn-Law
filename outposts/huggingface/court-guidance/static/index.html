03

Agent conduct

Rules say what applies. Conduct standards say how the system must behave.

Lawyers have professional rules. Judges have ethical canons. A court-facing AI system also needs public, versioned role standards. These are design commitments—not a claim that human codes automatically govern a model.

01

Truth before fluency

Never invent a rule, case, quotation, deadline, filing event, fact, or source.

02

Human authorship

Explain choices and consequences. Do not impersonate the person or choose their legal position.

03

Institutional neutrality

Do not favor the court, government, represented parties, repeat players, vendors, or the user.

04

Candor about role

Separate law, court guidance, explanation, example, model output, and legal advice.

05

Correction is a duty

Trace material claims, accept correction, preserve versions, and recheck every dependent workflow.

06

Privacy and access

Ask only for what is needed. Design for disability, language, low bandwidth, and a non-AI path.

04–05

Review, test, then pilot

Prove one narrow workflow before scaling.

The first beta should not try to know appellate law. It should perform one documented task well enough that court personnel and users can see every source, assumption, boundary, and correction.

Workflow simulationNo model · no case data · no legal answer
Person

Guidance assistant

I cannot calculate that from the information provided.

First I need the court, case type, filing, triggering event, event date, and any service facts the governing rules make relevant.

2 · Retrieve

3 · Explain

4 · Return control

Show the source-linked calculation and let the person review every input.

Release gates

  • AccuracyCan each claim be reproduced from a current official source?
  • ClarityCan a first-time user tell what is required and what is uncertain?
  • AgencyCan the user inspect, correct, decline, and choose what happens next?
  • NeutralityDo paired cases receive the same procedural care?
  • OperationsDoes the workflow reduce avoidable defects without hiding court burden?
  • AccessDo accessibility, language, privacy, and non-AI routes work at launch?

06 · The adoption beacon

Every technical choice returns to one institutional objective.

Not “does the demo sound impressive?” Not “can the model produce a polished filing?” The question is whether the whole system strengthens accurate, fair, understandable, and correctable participation in the court’s work.

The hard stop

If the tool makes polished filings less truthful, turns model confidence into false court authority, hides unequal errors, or makes rejection faster by making the person less visible, it fails.

Projected next phase · not live

A court-controlled local guidance assistant.

After the source packet, conduct layer, roles, and review gates are agreed, a local model can receive the complete approved context and serve one narrow workflow. It asks before assuming, cites before concluding, exposes uncertainty, and leaves all filing choices with the person.

This draft stops before that implementation on purpose. The interface comes after the court can see and control what would govern it.
